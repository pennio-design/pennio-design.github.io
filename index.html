The pattern

Why good businesses still look small.

Most of the businesses I work with already have a product people want. What holds them back is what the customer sees before they buy.

01
It starts and ends with a logo
A mark gets drawn before anyone asks what the business stands for, and that is the whole identity. Nothing says what the flyers, the labels or the posts are meant to look like.
02
Everything after it is made by whoever is free
The flyer from one person, the labels from the printer, the shop banner from the signwriter, the posts from a phone at night. Each picks their own colour and their own font.
03
The ads pay for the gap
You spend more and get fewer customers. The ad looks nothing like the page it sends people to, and the page reads like a template, so the click lands somewhere it does not trust.
04
Everything gets thrown out
Sales flatten, so the search for a fix starts back at the logo, and the flyers, the labels and the pages built around it get scrapped with it.
Your business does not look small because it is small.
It looks small because nothing matches.
Your logo, your flyers, your labels, your shop front and your posts were made by different people at different times, and none of them were told what the others looked like. The customer feels the gap even when she cannot name it. She reads it as a business that is not sure of itself, and she prices you accordingly.

Work

One case, told properly.

I would rather show you how one set of decisions was made than show you six logos with nothing behind them.

Brilla
Cleaning service · Brand identity and social design

A cleaning business that existed only as an idea. A service, no name people recognised, no packaging, no page, nothing to point a customer at.

What was going wrong

The founder had a real service and nothing to show for it. Every enquiry started from zero, because there was nothing a customer could look at and judge.

The question was not which logo to draw. It was what the business had to be before a logo could mean anything.

The question that shaped everything

Who pays for cleaning, and why do they choose one company over another?

Homes want it to feel safe. Offices want it to feel reliable. Those pull in different directions, and a brand that tries to be both ends up being neither.

The decision

We built for reliable. Offices buy on schedule and consistency, not on warmth, and that one choice set the name, the colour, the mark and the way the posts are written.

Every decision after this point had a reason you could argue with, which is the whole point of deciding first.

What I did

Positioning, the name, the mark, the colour and type system, and the social design system the business runs on.

What do you actually do?

I build brand identities: the name, the logo, the colours, the type, and the rules for using them. I build websites on top of that. I do not do one-off logos.

How much does it cost?

Brand identity starts at ₦350,000. Websites are priced after the call, once I know how many pages and what they have to do. You get a written price before any work starts.

I am just starting. Is this too early?

Sometimes. If you have not sold anything yet and you are not sure the idea works, spend the money on finding that out first. If you have customers and you want to charge more, this is the right time.

Do you work outside Nigeria?

Yes. Most of the work happens over calls and shared files.

I already have a logo and a website.

That is where most people start. On the call I look at what exists. If the foundation is solid I build on it instead of replacing it, and that costs you less.
