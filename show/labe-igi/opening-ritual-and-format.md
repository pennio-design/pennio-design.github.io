The elders answer:

> ọmọ 're, kaabo s'ábẹ igi olú **[confirm: the tone on the last vowel of *abẹ́*, and the tones on *kaabo*]**

> oó d'àgbà... **[confirm: the rest of the blessing]**

My reading, to be corrected: the child greets with respect; the elders say "good child, welcome under the mushroom tree"; and they bless the child ("you will grow into an elder"). *S'ábẹ́* is the same elision rule as the title (the preposition drops its vowel and the high tone lands on the *a*), which is the rule Paul taught.

## 2. What this changes

The show flips from **"elders interview a guest"** to **"a child asks, the elders answer."** That is better, for four reasons:

1. **The child is the èwe.** The series title, the logo (a child's hand holding the leaf) and the format now say the same thing.
2. **The greeting is the best 8 seconds the show has.** It carries cultural teaching (the greeting, the kneel and the prostration, the blessing) before any lecture. It is also the identical opening every episode, a ritual viewers will learn to wait for. On TikTok it is a hook that needs no explaining.
3. **It dramatises the language story.** The child's Yorùbá greeting is the ritual. The questions that follow may be in English or Pidgin. The elders **meet the child where he is and never correct his accent**, which is the show's own anti-shaming principle shown, not stated. By the end, the arc can bring more Yorùbá back into the child's mouth. That arc is a suggestion for Paul, not a decision.
4. **It keeps the show from being a lecture.** A child's questions are blunt and funny. Elders answering a child cannot talk down to an audience.

## 3. Where the real bearers and guests fit

The elders are the hosts. They do not know everything, and the show does not claim they do. When a question needs a real bearer (a historian, a Lanlate elder, a dyer), the elders **call on that person**: the bearer's recorded, consented audio is the answer, attributed to them by name (grade C in the claims tables). The source grades and dossier method in `research-and-scripting.md` still apply in full.

## 4. Revised episode shape

1. **The greeting** (8 to 16 seconds). Ritual, identical each episode.
2. **The question.** The child asks, in his own words.
3. **The answer.** The elders, then a bearer if needed.
4. **The proverb**, from whichever elder is silent.
5. **The child pushes back** ("but why?"). This is the stubborn, funny beat.
6. **The blessing and the close.** The child leaves with one thing to take away.

The earlier *tààrà* follow-up now belongs to the child: the direct question the elders cannot dodge.

## 5. Cast: rotating children

A different child each episode, **alternating boy and girl**, so both greetings appear across the season. Characters, invented, voiced by actors.

## 6. Child-safety rules (real children are different from characters)

1. **Characters only on screen.** No real child's face or likeness.
2. **If a real child's questions or voice are used, the guardian consents in writing**, the child can be anonymous, and no school, surname or location appears.
3. **Collect real questions** from children through families, schools or the comments ("what do you want to ask the tree?"), and animate them. The question belongs to the child. The voice can be an adult or a child actor.
4. TikTok's rules about minors apply. Check them before posting anything with a child's voice.
