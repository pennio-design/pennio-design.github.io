# Series: Ewé àti Èwe (working title)

Internal. **[Paul to confirm the title and every Yorùbá word.]** My reading: *ewé* is leaf, and *èwe* is youth, the young. Leaf and youth: the knowledge, and the one who inherits it. If you meant something different, this whole page changes.

## 1. The premise

A series inside L'ábẹ́ Igi about the **core skills of the ancient Yorùbá that the next generation needs to reawaken**. Each episode takes one skill and asks three things:

1. **The Art.** What was done, by whom, with what hands. (Practitioner at the table.)
2. **The Science.** What was actually happening, and what can be measured now. (A researcher at the table, or a documented study.)
3. **The Èwe.** Who is learning it today, and what would it take for a 12-year-old to pick it up. (A young person at the table, ideally one who already has.)

The pair, practitioner and èwe, is the series. The skill is useful, but the handover is the story.

## 2. Why "art and science" is the right frame

It stops the topic being nostalgia. The claim is not "our ancestors were wise". It is "here is a skill, here is what it does, here is the evidence, here is who is carrying it". Where the evidence is thin, the episode says so, and that gap is a call for the èwe to fill it.

## 3. Rules specific to this series

1. **No health claims.** Anything about ewé (herbs) is ethnobotany and published pharmacology, graded like every other claim. We never say a leaf cures, treats or prevents anything. Toxicity and interactions are stated where known. Every such post carries "This is not medical advice" and names a clinician as the place to go for treatment. Health content is also where TikTok and every platform is strictest.
2. **Consent and ownership of knowledge.** Practitioners, guilds and families hold this knowledge, sometimes as a livelihood or as a trust. The consent form covers what may be shown, what may not (some knowledge is held back by design), credit by name, and what happens if someone commercialises it. Do not record a method a practitioner has not agreed to publish. Look up existing frameworks on traditional knowledge before the first shoot.
3. **Sacred knowledge is not content.** Where a skill is tied to a rite or an orisha, the episode covers the craft and the public record, not the rite.
4. **Show hands.** An animated skill is not a skill. The series needs real footage or photographs of practice (with consent) alongside the animation, or the animation must be built from them. Do not animate a technique from my description.
5. **The same source grades apply** (A to D) from `research-and-scripting.md`.

## 5. Format

- **Long-form:** 8 to 12 minutes, three rounds as above.
- **TikTok cuts** (see `tiktok-first.md`): *The Art* (hands, 30 to 60 s), *The Science* (one measured fact, 30 s), *Meet the Èwe* (a young person doing it, 45 s).
- **Each episode leaves an asset**: a one-page skill card (what, why it matters, where to learn), which becomes part of the school pack.

## 6. A pilot for the series

**Recommend: indigo and adire.** It has living practitioners, a measurable science (the vat is a fermentation that can be tested), strong visuals that suit TikTok, no health risk, and no sacred material. It also pairs naturally with the PENNIO brand work, which is a point in its favour but not the reason.

Second: **counting**, because it is the lightest and the most personal.

Third: **ewé**, after the consent and health-claim rules have been tested on something safer.
