# Dossier v1: Ìbàràpá

Internal. Status: **first pass from web search, 24 November 2026. Not frozen.** For Paul to examine. Most web sources found are general or encyclopedic, so grades are cautious. Nothing here is a script line. Yorùbá spellings (Ìbàràpá, Igbó-Ọ̀rá and so on) need Paul's tone marks, and I have not added any I could not source.

## 1. The question (candidate)

Several are possible, to be chosen with Paul:
- *Where does a people's history live when the written record is thin?* (the Ibarapa case)
- *Ibarapa is known for twins, a clinic and a conflict. What is it known for to itself?*
- *Who tells Ibarapa's story: Oyo, Ibadan, Egba or Ibarapa?* (origin claims are contested, see section 4)

## 2. Why this is a show topic

What the search found is a place that is **known to outsiders through three narrow lenses**, twins, a university health programme and the 2021 attacks, while its own history (founding, migration, relationship with Oyo, Ibadan and Egba) is thinly documented in what is online. That gap is the show.

## 4. Conflicts, shown, not smoothed

- **Who are the Ibarapa people, historically?** The "from Old Oyo" account is the usual one. A political argument raised in the debate over joining Ogun State said most Ibarapa hail from Egba neighbours. That is a **political claim, not an established finding**. A scholar should settle it, not a script.
- **Founding of the towns**: only Eruwa has a detailed account online, from an Oyo-origin tradition. No well-sourced account of Igangan, Idere, Tapa or Aiyete's founding was found.
- **Casualty figures for June 2021**: three different numbers, three different sources.
- **Ijaye war date**: 1860, 1861 or 1862 depending on source.

## 7. Sensitivities

- **The 2021 attacks involve deaths, ethnic tension and live politics.** This show should not be the place that inflames. Decide deliberately whether it appears at all, and if so, only through people who were there and agree.
- **Origin claims are political** (Oyo, Ibadan, Egba, Ogun State). A wrong line can offend a town or a king.
- **Oral traditions belong to families and towns.** A guest's account is theirs, attributed to them.
- **Twin lore** touches on belief. Some communities honour twins in particular ways. Treat with respect, not as a curiosity.

## C. Updated gaps

- **Ibarapa's own dialect has no documentation I could find.** The only way to record it is from speakers, which is exactly what the show can do.
- No source on accent shaming or dialect stigma. This will need people's own stories (grade C) plus any sociolinguistic study a researcher at a university can point to.
- No documented account of the land gift between Lanlate and Eruwa from a second source.
- How Ibarapa came under Ibadan and Oyo State.

## D. What this changes

1. **Lanlate's hills and their protective role** are a real, specific story that a Lanlate elder can tell. It is a better episode seed than the generic origin question.
2. **The Eruwa and Lanlate land gift** is the most concrete, human piece of Ibarapa history so far. It needs two tellers, one from each town.
3. **The Egba and Oyo origin accounts both stay.** The show does not pick. It lets each be told, by a named person, and brings a historian to say what the written record supports.
