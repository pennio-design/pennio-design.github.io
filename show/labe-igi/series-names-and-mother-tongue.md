# Series: Names, dialects and the mother tongue (working title)

Internal. **[Paul to supply the Yorùbá title and confirm every Yorùbá word.]** Written from Paul's brief on 24 November 2026.

## 1. Purpose, in Paul's words (paraphrased)

- Ask a series of questions on **names** and the **sources of names**.
- Bring out the **beauty of the dialects**, which are being slowly lost because people want to sound modern and polished.
- **End accent shaming among Yorùbá people.**
- **Strengthen the use of the Yorùbá language** at home, as Yorùbá children now often speak Pidgin and English there. Paul sees this as the result of the mother tongue being demoted over time, not an accident.

## 2. The argument the series makes, and what it must prove

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Children are moving to English and Pidgin at home | Paul's observation (C). Studies exist, such as the Lagos study in the Ibarapa dossier (S3, B), but none found measures Pidgin by generation. **Need a better source or a researcher at the table.** |
| This is demotion, not accident | An interpretation. It stays Paul's argued position, attributed, unless a researcher supports it. |
| Dialects are being lost to sound "modern and polished" | Paul's observation. **No evidence of dialect stigma found yet.** Collect first-person accounts (C) and ask a sociolinguist. |
| Accent shaming exists and does harm | Same. First-person accounts, then a scholar's view. |

The show's strongest asset here is real people telling their own stories of being corrected or mocked for their dialect, with consent. A script from me cannot supply that.

## 3. A term to rethink

Paul used "bastardization" and "vernacularization". For the show:
- **"Bastardization"** is an insult word with its own harm, and platforms and some viewers will react to it before the argument. A plainer word (erosion, displacement, demotion) says the same thing without that cost. **Paul's decision.**
- **"Vernacularization"** may mislead, because "vernacular" normally just means a local language. What Paul means is closer to **making the mother tongue the lesser language**. A listener who hears "vernacular" may hear the opposite. Pick a phrase and define it on screen.

## 5. How dialects get documented

This is where the show becomes an **archive**, not only a conversation:
- Record each guest speaking in their own dialect, with consent and archival rights.
- Subtitle in Standard Yorùbá and English, so the dialect is heard, not corrected.
- Never "correct" a guest's dialect on screen.
- Consider depositing recordings with a university linguistics department.

## 6. Safety and respect

- **Don't turn the series into mockery** in either direction. The aim is to end accent shaming, so the show cannot shame anyone's Pidgin or English either. Pidgin is a language people use for real reasons.
- **Families and towns own their histories.** Lanlate's and Eruwa's accounts are theirs, attributed.
- **No Yorùbá line airs without Paul's review.** Dialect spellings especially: there is no single standard for writing many dialect forms, so the guest or Paul decides.
