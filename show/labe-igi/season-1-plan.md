# L'ábẹ́ Igi: Season 1 plan

Internal. **TikTok-first revision in `tiktok-first.md` supersedes the schedule and episode length below.** Yorùbá strings are marked **[Paul to confirm]** until signed off. Words Paul has supplied (yékéyéké, sàn-án, tààrà, gidigidi) are used as given.

## 1. What season 1 has to prove

Not reach. Three things:
1. **The look holds.** Baba Olú, Mama Olú and the tree stay consistent across six episodes in Flow.
2. **The Yorùbá holds.** Voice and pronunciation pass your ear, every episode.
3. **The format earns a sponsor.** One episode someone would put their name on.

Anything else (followers, views) is a result, not a goal. I will not set invented targets. Measure the first three episodes and set targets from that.

## 3. The six episodes

Each episode has one deep question and one **ayán word** that carries the language moment. The word is chosen to rhyme with the question.

| # | Question | Ayán word of the episode | Why this word |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who should teach a child their mother tongue: school, parent or a machine? | *l'ábẹ́* (ní + abẹ́ elision) | The title itself. Episode 1 explains the show's name. |
| 2 | If a machine can answer anything, what is a teacher for? | *yékéyéké*: *ó ti yé mi yékéyéké* | There is a degree of understanding. A machine gives you the answer. A teacher gets you to *yékéyéké*. |
| 3 | Can a proverb be data? | *sàn-án* | Exactness. A proverb loses its meaning when it is only approximately remembered. |
| 4 | Whose history does the machine know? | *tààrà* | Straight, direct. Who tells it straight, and who bends it. |
| 5 | What should a 12-year-old in Lagos, Kumasi or Kigali learn that we do not teach? | *gidigidi* | Emphasis. What deserves "very, very much". |
| 6 | Apprenticeship or degree: which one survives? | Paul to choose from the same family | Closing the season on a word he picks himself. |

The language moment is the show's differentiator, and each one can run as a stand-alone 20-second clip.

## 4. Episode structure (unchanged)

1. Cold open and ritual line, *Dáhùn ìbéèrè ìjìnlẹ̀*.
2. The Story.
3. The Proverb (the silent elder).
4. The language moment (20 seconds).
5. *Tààrà*, the direct follow-up.
6. The close.

Rule: every episode has one moment where the guest is funny, wrong or stubborn.

## 6. Guests: how to move from characters to real people

1. Episodes 1 and 2 use labelled characters.
2. Draft a one-page consent and release form before anyone is recorded. It states that voice is used, that the guest is shown as an animated character, and that they approve their episode before release.
3. Show each guest their animated self before release. A face they dislike is a reason to redo it.
4. Never put words in a real guest's mouth. Trims are allowed. Rewrites are not.
5. Source guests from people who already know you: teachers, builders, parents, students.

## 7. What each episode produces (one shoot, many assets)

- 1 long episode (8 to 12 minutes).
- 3 to 5 vertical clips: the question, the proverb, the language moment, the *tààrà* exchange.
- 1 discussion guide (one page: the question, the proverb, three prompts for a classroom or family).
- 1 proverb or ayán card (a shareable still).

Six episodes make a **school pack** of six guides and six cards. This is the sellable asset, and the reason education was the right theme.

## 8. Money, in order of likelihood

1. **A sponsored episode.** A brand sponsors the table, the tree or the question. Sell it only after episode 3. Show them the finished work.
2. **The school pack.** Episodes plus guides for teachers, tutors and learning centres. Price it after you know the production cost per episode.
3. **PENNIO clients.** The show shows your content-engine method working. Use it as proof, and only as proof you can show.
4. **Ilé Eré.** List it as a title on the platform.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The Yorùbá is wrong on screen | Nothing airs unreviewed. Add a second Yorùbá reader if you can. |
| Generated speech mispronounces tone | Test on day one. Fall back to recorded voices. |
| Flow drifts across shots | Locked cameras, reference images in every shot, short clips. |
| Sacred register offends | Never depict a real office, rite or orisha. White cloth and manner only. |
| Guests uncomfortable with being animated | Show them first. Consent before release. |
| One person is the bottleneck | Second voice, and a Yorùbá-literate reviewer. Paul stays the final reviewer. |
| The name collides with the Studio | Àyàn (drum, the Studio room) and ayán (qualifying word) are different words. Keep them apart in copy. |
| Education turns preachy | Entertainment first. The funny, wrong or stubborn moment is mandatory. |
