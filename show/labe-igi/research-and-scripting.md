# L'ábẹ́ Igi: research and scripting method

Internal. This is the core of the show. The language moments from earlier files are a garnish. The main work is **conversations about culture, society and history, built on facts that are at risk of disappearing from documentation.**

## 1. The problem with this show

The facts most worth recording are the ones least documented. That is also where a researcher, human or AI, is most likely to get it wrong. So:

1. **Nothing is stated as fact unless it carries a source.** Every date, name, number and quote in a script sits in a claims table with where it came from.
2. **What cannot be sourced becomes a question, not a statement.** The elder asks "I was told that...? Is it so?" and a named guest confirms or corrects. The guest is then the source, attributed by name.
3. **I will not invent a statistic, a quote or a date.** If I cannot find it, I say so and leave a gap.

## 2. The guest is the archive

Where the written record is thin, the person at the table is the documentation. That moves real guests, elders, historians, craftspeople, custodians of oríkì, earlier than I planned. Consent covers recording, animated portrayal, **and archival use** (deposit with a library or university, if the guest agrees). Characters are only for episodes where the subject is the question, not the facts.

## 3. Source grades (used in every claims table)

| Grade | Meaning | How the script may use it |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Two or more independent credible sources agree (peer-reviewed work, an archive, a museum, a recognised body such as UNESCO) | May be stated by the host |
| **B** | One credible source | May be stated, attributed ("according to...") |
| **C** | Oral account from a named bearer | Stated by the guest, attributed to them, flagged as oral |
| **D** | Unverified, or sources conflict | A question only. Never a statement. Paul decides whether it stays. |

Figures always carry: number, unit, year, source and page or URL.

## 4. The dossier (one per episode, before any script)

1. **The question** (the deep question the elders ask).
2. **Why this is disappearing** (what is lost, who holds it, what has been written).
3. **Claims table** (claim, grade, source, date accessed, notes).
4. **Conflicts** (where sources disagree, shown, not smoothed over).
5. **Candidate guests** (name, why them, how to reach them, what they would confirm).
6. **Sensitivities** (sacred, political, family or community claims where someone could be wronged).
7. **What I could not find** (the honest gaps).

## 5. Workflow

1. **Claude** finds sources and writes the dossier.
2. **Paul** examines the dossier, strikes or corrects, and adds what he knows that is not written down.
3. **Claude** writes the script from the frozen dossier only: conversation flow, host questions, the proverb slot, the *tààrà* question, a claims table at the end.
4. **Paul** examines the script, line by line, including every Yorùbá string.
5. **Freeze.** No changes after Paul's sign-off, except corrections.
6. **Then** Flow prompts. Never before.

Version every file (`dossier-v1`, `script-v1`) and keep Paul's marks in the next version.
