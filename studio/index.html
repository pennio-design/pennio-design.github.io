Your voice

Record on your phone's voice recorder, or pick any audio file: a hum, a chant, a guitar line, a market sound. The studio cleans it up and cuts it into pieces you can play on the grid.

No recording yet. The Vox lane stays silent until you add one.

Remix

Each tap writes a new variation: drum hits move, the dùndún line shifts, and your vocal chops are reordered. Downbeats stay put so the groove holds. Undo, at the top, walks back any change, one step at a time.

Mix

Ìlù: the drum speaks your voice

Speak or sing into your recorder and add it above. The studio follows the rise and fall of your voice and plays it on the dùndún, the way a drummer carries speech. Each syllable also lands on the dùndún row as high, mid or low, relative to your own voice.

Add a recording first.

Type it plain. Say it once. Get the tones.

Your voice decides the tone marks. Type a word or short phrase without marks, record yourself saying it, and the studio writes it with the tones you spoke. Tap any syllable to correct it.

Step 1 · once

Calibrate your voice

Sing do, re, mi slowly, with a short pause between each note, and add the recording. This tells the studio where your low, mid and high sit. You can skip it, but results are steadier with it.

Not calibrated
Step 2

Type without tone marks

Plain letters are fine. Type ẹ, ọ and ṣ if you can: the voice decides high, mid and low, while the dots below are vowel and consonant sounds the studio does not detect yet.

Step 3

Say it and add the recording

Say the phrase at a normal pace, close to the phone, somewhere quiet. Short phrases work best.
