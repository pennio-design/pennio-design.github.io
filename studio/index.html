Ìlù: the drum speaks your voice

Speak or sing into your recorder and add it above. The studio follows the rise and fall of your voice and plays it on the dùndún, the way a drummer carries speech. Each syllable also lands on the dùndún row as high, mid or low, relative to your own voice.

Step 1

Type without tone marks

Plain letters are fine. Your voice decides high, mid and low. Once it has learned your vowels, it also decides e or ẹ and o or ọ. Type ṣ yourself: the studio cannot hear s from ṣ yet. Anything you type with a dot stays as you typed it.

Step 2

Say it and add the recording

Say the phrase at a normal pace, close to the phone, somewhere quiet. Short phrases work best.

Optional · about a minute, onceCalibrate your voice for steadier tones

Sing do, re, mi slowly, with a short pause between each note, and add the recording. This tells the studio where your low, mid and high sit. You can skip it, but results are steadier with it.

Not calibrated

Then say the seven vowels, one at a time with a short pause: a · e · ẹ · i · o · ọ · u. This teaches the studio your e and ẹ, o and ọ, so it can add the dots below from your voice.
