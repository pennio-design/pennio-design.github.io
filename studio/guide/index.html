How to use pennio.studio

Everything the studio does, in the order you will want it: a first beat, your own voice, the talking drum, Yoruba with its tones, and where your work is kept.

01Before you start

Open pennio.agency/studio on your phone or laptop. Chrome, Safari and Edge all work. The first time, you add your name and email and pick what you will make. There is no password and nothing to install.

  • Your beats, recordings and written words save in the browser you are using, on that device. Open the studio in a different browser or on another phone and you start fresh there.
  • Turn your phone's volume up and, on iPhone, flip the silent switch off. iPhones mute the studio while the switch is on.
  • Use headphones when you record over a beat, so the drums do not leak into your voice.

02Your first beat

The studio opens on Àyàn, the music studio. The grid has one row per drum and one square per step. A lit square plays when the playhead passes it.

  1. Tap a starter groove under the controls, such as Afro pulse or Standard bell 12/8.
  2. Press the orange play button. Press it again to stop.
  3. Tap any square to add a hit. Tap the same square again to change it, and keep tapping to clear it. On the dùndún row the taps cycle through H high, M mid and L low. On the bàtá, agogo, ṣẹ̀kẹ̀rẹ̀ and udu rows the second tap gives a softer or second sound.
  4. Drag Tempo to speed up or slow down. Drag Swing to make the groove lean and sway.
  5. Switch between 12 and 16 steps. Twelve suits the bell patterns that count in threes; sixteen suits most modern grooves.
  6. Tap a drum's name on the left to mute it. Tap again to bring it back.
Made a mistake? Undo sits next to Clear and takes back any change, one step at a time, including Clear itself.

On a phone the grid scrolls sideways. Swipe the grid left to reach the later steps.

03Bring your voice

Your voice, a hum, a chant, a guitar line or a sound from the market can all go into the studio.

  1. Put on headphones. In Your voice, tap ● Record and allow the microphone. With Record over the beat on, the screen counts 4, 3, 2, 1 in time, the groove starts, and you sing over it. Tap ■ Stop when you finish. Recordings stop on their own at 60 seconds.
  2. Prefer your phone's voice recorder? Record there, then tap Add recording and pick the file. .m4a, .mp3 and .wav all work.
  3. The studio cleans the recording up straight away: it cuts low rumble and muddiness, lifts clarity, evens out the level and adds a light room sound. Switch between Before and Enhanced to hear the difference.
  4. Tap Preview to hear the recording on its own.

Recording on the beat

Phones and Bluetooth earphones delay sound a little, which can make a voice land late. Fix it once:

  1. Take your headphones off and turn the volume up.
  2. Tap Measure my delay and stay quiet for three seconds while it plays a few clicks.
  3. The studio remembers your delay and places every recording on the beat. Measure again if you change phones or earphones.

Switch off Count-in or Click if you prefer to record without them, and switch off Record over the beat to record on your own.

04A beat from your voice

Sing or speak something with a rhythm, add it, then tap Make a beat from my voice.

  • The studio finds the tempo of what you sang and sets the beat to it.
  • It finds the note your voice sits around and tunes the udu and dùndún to it, so the drums sit in your key.
  • The bàtá fills the gaps where you pause, and the dùndún answers with the tones of your own voice.

Your full vocal switches on in Layers over the new beat. Tap the button again for a different take. Undo brings back the one before.

Tempo feels twice too fast or too slow? Drag Tempo to double or halve it. Both readings fit the same rhythm, and the studio cannot always tell which one you meant.

05Ìlù: the drum speaks your voice

Yoruba is a tonal language, and the dùndún talking drum carries those tones. Ìlù listens to the rise and fall of your voice and plays it back on the drum.

  1. Add a recording of yourself speaking or singing in Yoruba. Speak clearly, one voice, somewhere quiet.
  2. Tap Translate my voice to drum. Each syllable appears as high, mid or low, measured against your own voice, and lands on the dùndún row.
  3. Tap Hear the drum alone to listen to the full drum line.
  4. In Layers, switch on Talking drum to play that drum line over the beat. It can play together with your full vocal.

Try "Ojú gbooro", meaning a wide view, then "Ojú gbòòrò", the greeting to hairdressers at work. Same letters, different tones, and the drum plays them differently.

06Remix, layers and export

Remix

Each tap of Remix writes a new version: drum hits move, the dùndún line shifts and your vocal chops change order. Hits on the main beats stay where they are so the groove holds. The version number counts up as you go, and Undo walks back.

Layers

Every sound in your track has a row in Layers: the six drums, Vox chops (pieces of your recording on the grid), Full vocal and Talking drum.

  • The switch turns a layer on or off.
  • Volume runs from 0 to 150. Pull a drum down to make room for your voice.
  • Tune moves a drum or the Vox chops up or down by up to 12 semitones, so the drums sit in your key.
  • Timing on Full vocal slides your voice earlier or later by up to 150 milliseconds if it still feels off the beat.

Export

Tap Export WAV to save the groove as a .wav file. It holds four rounds of the pattern, or the full length of your vocal if that runs longer. Muted drums stay silent in the export.

07Write Yoruba with its tones

Typing ẹ́ or ọ̀ on a phone is slow. In the Write tab you type plain letters and your voice supplies the tone marks.

  1. Calibrate once. Sing do, re, mi slowly with a short pause between each note and add the recording. This shows the studio where your low, mid and high sit.
  2. Type a word or short phrase without tone marks, for example oju gbooro. Type ẹ, ọ and ṣ if you can: the voice decides tones, while the dots below are sounds the studio does not detect yet.
  3. Say it at a normal pace, record it, and add the recording.
  4. The phrase comes back with its tone marks. Tap any syllable to correct it. Where a word has several meanings, such as ìlù (drum) and ìlú (town), pick the one you meant from the list.
  5. Tap Copy to paste it anywhere, or Play on dùndún to hear it on the drum.

The studio writes standard Yoruba. Tones differ between dialects, so check anything you will publish.

08Where your work lives

The studio saves as you work. Close the page, come back tomorrow, and it opens where you left off.

Your beatsTap My work at the top of Àyàn. Every project is listed with the date it last changed and whether it has a recording. Tap Open to go back to one.
A new projectMy work → New project. Rename it in the name field at the top of Àyàn.
Your recordingsSaved inside the project you made them in, and they come back when you open it.
Your written wordsIn the Write tab under Your words. Tap one to copy it again.
Undo historyCovers the project you have open. It starts over when you open another project.
Your name and emailMy work → Edit my details.
DeletingIn My work, tap Delete and then tap again to confirm.

Keep a backup

Everything lives in this browser on this device. Clearing your browser data, using a private window, or a browser tidying up sites you have not visited in a while can remove it. PENNIO cannot see or recover work saved in your browser. Your backup file is the only copy outside it, so the studio reminds you after each new recording and once a week.

  1. Open My work and tap Download backup. You get one file holding every project, recording and written word.
  2. Keep that file somewhere safe, such as Google Drive or your email.
  3. To bring your work back, on any device, open My work, tap Restore backup and pick the file.

09Ojú Gbòòrò casting

Ojú Gbòòrò is a hair culture brand bringing the greeting to hairdressers back into everyday life, and bringing African hair back as culture. A ń wá onídìrí ìbílẹ̀ tó dáńgájíá: we are looking for skilled traditional hairdressers, and for models to wear their work.

The entry form is at pennio.agency/oju-gbooro, linked from the bottom of the studio. Pick Hairdresser, Model or Both, add how to reach you and a link to your photos, and send. Entries are open to people aged 18 or older.

10When something goes wrong

  • No sound. Turn the volume up, flip the iPhone silent switch off, and tap play again.
  • Record does nothing. Your browser has blocked the microphone. Allow it in the browser's site settings, or use your phone's recorder and Add recording.
  • "That file could not be read". Save the recording as .m4a, .mp3 or .wav and try again.
  • The drum reads the wrong tone. Record closer to the phone in a quieter room, calibrate in the Write tab, and correct syllables by tapping them.
  • My work is gone. You may be in a different browser, a private window, or your browser data was cleared. Restore your latest backup from My work. Without a backup file, PENNIO has no copy to recover it from.

Still stuck? Email contact@pennio.agency and say what you tapped and what happened.
