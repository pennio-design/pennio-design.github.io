How to use pennio.studio

Everything the studio does, in the order you will want it: a first beat, your own voice, the talking drum, Yoruba with its tones, and where your work is kept.

01Before you start

Open pennio.agency/studio on your phone or laptop. Chrome, Safari and Edge all work. The first time, you add your name and email and pick what you will make. There is no password and nothing to install.

  • Your beats, recordings and written words save in the browser you are using. Cloud saving, which keeps them in your PENNIO account for any device, is switching on soon.
  • Turn your phone's volume up and, on iPhone, flip the silent switch off. iPhones mute the studio while the switch is on.
  • Use headphones when you record over a beat, so the drums do not leak into your voice.

02Your first beat

The studio opens on Àyàn, the music studio. The grid has one row per drum and one square per step. A lit square plays when the playhead passes it.

  1. Tap a starter groove under the controls. There are ten: Afro pulse, Standard bell 12/8, Highlife sway, Afrobeats, Afrobeat (Fela), Jùjú, Fújì, Àpàlà, Amapiano and Talking drum call. They are starting points in each style, not copies of any song. Once you have a melody, a new groove swaps only the drums and keeps your melody.
  2. Press the drum face at the left of the player bar: it is the play button. Press it again to stop. While the beat plays, the drum head ripples on every hit, and the bar number, the beat and the current chord show beside it.
  3. Tap any square to add a hit. Tap the same square again to change it, and keep tapping to clear it. On the dùndún row the taps cycle through H high, M mid and L low. On the bàtá, agogo, ṣẹ̀kẹ̀rẹ̀ and udu rows the second tap gives a softer or second sound.
  4. Drag Tempo to speed up or slow down. Drag Swing to make the groove lean and sway.
  5. Switch between 12 and 16 steps. Twelve suits the bell patterns that count in threes; sixteen suits most modern grooves.
  6. Tap a drum's name on the left to mute it. Tap again to bring it back.

Melody and chords

Below the drums are three rows that play notes, all locked to the key you choose, so nothing sounds wrong.

  • Pick a Key, then Minor or Major, then a set of Chords. The chords move one per bar, and the row of chord names lights up as each one plays.
  • Bass plays the root of the chord. Tap again for the fifth (5), and again for the octave above (8).
  • Keys plays the whole chord: a short hit, or tap again for a chord held across half the bar.
  • Guitar plays clean highlife picking on the notes of whichever chord is playing: R is the root, then 3, 5, 8 (the root an octave up) and 10. Because it follows the chords, it never clashes.
  • Kalimba (a thumb piano) plays a melody on five notes that suit the key. Taps cycle through notes 1 to 5.
  • Flute works the same way, an octave higher, with soft held notes. Use it for a second line that answers the kalimba or your voice.
Made a mistake? Undo sits next to Clear and takes back any change, one step at a time, including Clear itself.

On a phone the grid scrolls sideways. Swipe the grid left to reach the later steps.

03Bring your voice

Your voice, a hum, a chant, a guitar line or a sound from the market can all go into the studio.

  1. Put on headphones. In Your voice, tap ● Record and allow the microphone. With Record over the beat on, the screen counts 4, 3, 2, 1 in time, the groove starts, and you sing over it. Tap ■ Stop when you finish. Recordings stop on their own at 60 seconds.
  2. Prefer your phone's voice recorder? Record there, then tap Add recording and pick the file. .m4a, .mp3 and .wav all work.
  3. The studio cleans the recording up straight away. It learns the background noise from the quiet moments and removes it, evens out loud and quiet phrases, then shapes the voice and sets its loudness. In our tests it lowered steady hiss by 10 to 13 dB. Switch between Before and Enhanced to hear the difference.
  4. Voice style picks the finish: Natural keeps it plain, Studio (the default) adds warmth and a small room, Radio is close and punchy, Afro vocal adds brightness and a slap echo, Hall adds a big space. Switching style is quick, because the noise removal is done once.
  5. Tap Preview to hear the recording on its own. Once a recording is in, a box under the buttons shows Your recording with its length. Re-record records a new take that replaces it. Delete, tapped twice, removes it and keeps the beat. The same two buttons sit on the Full vocal row in Layers.

Recording on the beat

Phones and Bluetooth earphones delay sound a little, which can make a voice land late. Fix it once:

  1. Take your headphones off and turn the volume up.
  2. Tap Measure my delay and stay quiet for three seconds while it plays a few clicks.
  3. The studio remembers your delay and places every recording on the beat. Measure again if you change phones or earphones.

During the count-in bar the groove already plays, so you hear the beat before you come in. Switch off Beat in the count-in if you want clicks only.

Switch off Count-in or Click if you prefer to record without them, and switch off Record over the beat to record on your own.

If the beat is silent on your phone after you allow the microphone, the phone paused the sound. The studio tells you; tap play once, then record again.

04A beat from your voice

Sing or speak something with a rhythm, add it, then tap Make a beat from my voice.

  • The studio listens for where each syllable and hit starts, finds the steady pulse in them, and sets the tempo to it.
  • It puts beat one where you first come in strongly, so the bar starts with you. Anything you sing before that is treated as a pickup.
  • It finds the note your voice sits around and sets the key from it, so the udu, dùndún, bass and keys all sit in your key.
  • The bàtá fills the gaps where you pause, and the dùndún answers with the tones of your own voice.

Your full vocal switches on in Layers over the new beat. Tap the button again for a different take. Undo brings back the one before.

Beat not sitting with your voice? Tap Tap along. Your recording plays: tap the button on every beat, starting from the first strong one. After 12 taps, or when the recording ends, the studio sets the tempo and beat one from your taps and builds the beat again.
Tempo feels twice too fast or too slow? Drag Tempo to double or halve it. Both readings fit the same rhythm, and the studio cannot always tell which one you meant.

05Ìlù: the drum speaks your voice

Yoruba is a tonal language, and the dùndún talking drum carries those tones. Ìlù listens to the rise and fall of your voice and plays it back on the drum.

  1. Add a recording of yourself speaking or singing in Yoruba. Speak clearly, one voice, somewhere quiet.
  2. Tap Translate my voice to drum. Each syllable appears as high, mid or low, measured against your own voice, and lands on the dùndún row.
  3. Tap Hear the drum alone to listen to the full drum line.
  4. In Layers, switch on Talking drum to play that drum line over the beat. It can play together with your full vocal.

Try "ìlù", a drum, then "ìlú", a town. Same letters, different tones, and the drum plays them differently.

06Remix, layers and export

Remix

Each tap of Remix writes a new version: drum hits move, the dùndún line shifts and your vocal chops change order. Hits on the main beats stay where they are so the groove holds. The version number counts up as you go, and Undo walks back.

Layers

Every sound in your track has a row in Layers: the six drums, Bass, Keys, Guitar, Kalimba, Flute, Vox chops (pieces of your recording on the grid), Full vocal and Talking drum.

  • The switch turns a layer on or off.
  • Volume runs from 0 to 150. Pull a drum down to make room for your voice.
  • Tune moves a drum, an instrument or the Vox chops up or down by up to 12 semitones.
  • Timing on Full vocal slides your voice earlier or later by up to 150 milliseconds if it still feels off the beat.

Move around your song

Once you have a recording, a row appears under the player: back to the start, back one bar, a slider you can drag to any point, forward one bar, and Loop. The slider shows the bar and the time. On a keyboard, Left and Right arrows move one bar.

Loop works in two taps: tap it at the bar where the loop should start, move to the bar where it should end, and tap it again. The studio repeats those bars until you tap Loop a third time to clear it. Use it to work on one verse or one line.

What's playing

Only one thing plays at a time: the beat, your voice preview, or the talking drum alone. Starting one stops the others. While anything plays, a Now playing bar at the bottom of the screen names it, and its Stop button silences everything.

Install it as an app

On Android and in Chrome on a computer, the studio shows a Get the app card. Tap Install and pennio.studio goes on your home screen with its own icon. It opens full screen and still opens without data. Your work carries over, because the app and Chrome share the same storage.

On iPhone, open the studio in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. The iPhone app keeps its own copy of your work, separate from Safari, so download a backup from Sessions first and restore it inside the app.

Export

Tap Export to open the export box. The track holds four rounds of the pattern, or the full length of your vocal if that runs longer. Muted sounds stay silent.

  • MP3 is a small file, right for WhatsApp, Instagram and sending to friends. WAV is full quality, for a producer or another music app.
  • Match loudness is on by default. It sets your track to about -14 LUFS, the level Spotify, YouTube and Apple Music play at, so it does not sound quieter than other songs. Turn it off to keep the mix exactly as it plays in the studio.
  • Download saves the file to your phone. Share to WhatsApp opens your phone's share sheet with the track attached: pick WhatsApp, then the chat. If your browser cannot share files, the track downloads and WhatsApp opens, so attach it with the paperclip.

07Write Yoruba with its tones

Typing ẹ́ or ọ̀ on a phone is slow. In the Write tab you type plain letters and your voice supplies the tone marks.

  1. Calibrate once. Sing do, re, mi slowly with a short pause between each note and add the recording. This shows the studio where your low, mid and high sit.
  2. Type a word or short phrase without tone marks, for example oju gbooro. Type ẹ, ọ and ṣ if you can: the voice decides tones, while the dots below are sounds the studio does not detect yet.
  3. Say it at a normal pace, record it, and add the recording.
  4. The phrase comes back with its tone marks. Tap any syllable to correct it. Where a word has several meanings, such as ìlù (drum) and ìlú (town), pick the one you meant from the list.
  5. Tap Copy to paste it anywhere, or Play on dùndún to hear it on the drum.

The studio writes standard Yoruba. Tones differ between dialects, so check anything you will publish.

Dots below from your voice

In step 1, after do re mi, record the seven vowels one at a time with a short pause: a, e, ẹ, i, o, ọ, u. The studio learns how your e differs from your ẹ, and your o from your ọ. From then on, when you type a plain e or o, your voice decides whether it gets a dot below. Anything you type with a dot stays as you typed it, and words in the wordlist keep their dictionary spelling. The studio cannot hear s from ṣ yet, so type ṣ yourself.

Tone game

The Tone game in Write trains your ear and voice. Sing do re mi once so it learns your low, mid and high, then say what it shows: àá, áà, aa and so on. Hear it hums the pattern first. The bars turn green where you matched and red where you missed. Five right opens the next level: two sounds on a, then all seven vowels, then three sounds, then real words like ọkọ́ and ọkọ̀. Your score, streak and levels stay in this browser.

The game judges the whole pattern, the way a listener does, so it does not matter if your speaking voice sits higher or lower than your do re mi, or drifts down as you speak. It learns where your speaking voice sits from every answer it accepts, so it gets steadier after a few turns. If it marks you wrong but your rise and fall matched, tap I said it right: it counts the turn and adjusts to your voice. Singing do re mi again starts that learning over.

Ogbifọ̀: translate a line

Under Write, the Ogbifọ̀ box takes a line in Yoruba or English and works out which language it is on your phone, then sets the translation to the other one. Mixed lines are read as Yoruba. If it guesses wrong, tap Swap.

  • Speak turns your voice into text as a first draft, on phones that support it. Correct the words before you translate.
  • Use my Write result brings in the tone-marked line you just made above.
  • Live translation is switching on soon. Until then, write your own translation and tap Save the pair. Saved pairs stay in this browser, and Copy both gives you the two lines for a caption.

Songwriting hacks

Further down the Write tab. Give me a prompt gives you a topic, a thing to write and a rule, for days when nothing comes. Will it fit the beat? counts the syllables in each line you type and tells you roughly how many bars it needs at your tempo, at two syllables a beat. Yoruba words count every vowel and a syllabic n; other words are counted by their vowel sounds, so treat the number as a guide. Below that are twelve hacks, such as writing the hook first, letting the melody follow the tones, and call and response. Tap one to read it.

·Walkthroughs

Tap Learn at the top of the studio for short walkthroughs that point at each button and wait for you to tap it: Your first beat, A beat from your voice, Melody and chords, Sessions, history and backups and Write Yoruba with tones. The first time you open the studio, Show me around starts the first one.

08Where your work lives

The studio saves as you work. Close the page, come back tomorrow, and it opens where you left off.

Your sessionsTap Sessions at the top of Àyàn. Your current session is at the top, with how long you have worked on it. Previous sessions lists the rest, newest first, with the date, time spent, tempo and key. Tap Continue to pick one up again.
Start new sessionSessions → Start new session. The one you were on moves to Previous sessions. Rename a session in the name field at the top of Àyàn.
HistoryTap History at the top of Àyàn. Every session keeps checkpoints: when you open it, every few minutes while you work, before a preset, a beat from your voice or deleting a recording, and when you leave. Save your own with a name, like "verse idea". Go back here returns the session to that point, saving where you are first. Copy as new session starts a fresh session from it.
Your recordingsSaved inside the session you made them in, and in its checkpoints, so going back brings the recording back too.
Your written wordsIn the Write tab under Your words. Tap one to copy it again.
Undo and RedoStep back and forward through your last changes in the session you have open. For anything older, use History.
Your name and emailSessions → Edit my details.
DeletingIn Sessions, tap Delete on a previous session and then tap again to confirm. Its checkpoints go with it.

Cloud saving (switching on soon)

Cloud saving will keep your projects, recordings and written words in your PENNIO account. There is no password. Until it switches on, download a backup from Sessions. Once it is on, it works like this:

  1. Open Sessions and find Cloud saving. Check your email address and tap Send sign-in link.
  2. Open the email. Tap the link on the same device, or type the code from the email into the studio and tap Sign in.
  3. From then on, every change saves to your account a few seconds after you make it. The top of the studio shows Saved to cloud.

On a new phone or computer, open pennio.agency/studio, tap I have used pennio.studio before: restore my work, and sign in with the same email. Your projects and recordings come back.

Your cloud copy is stored with Supabase in Stockholm, Sweden, and only you can open it. Delete my cloud copy in Sessions removes it, and leaves the copy in your browser alone.

Keep a backup

Everything lives in this browser on this device. Clearing your browser data, using a private window, or a browser tidying up sites you have not visited in a while can remove it. Without cloud saving, PENNIO cannot see or recover work saved in your browser. Your backup file is then the only copy outside it, so the studio reminds you after each new recording and once a week.

  1. Open Sessions and tap Download backup. You get one file holding every session, its checkpoints, recordings and written words.
  2. Keep that file somewhere safe, such as Google Drive or your email.
  3. To bring your work back, on any device, open Sessions, tap Restore a backup file and pick the file from your downloads, Drive or email. Before anything is restored, the studio lists the sessions inside the file and ticks the ones that are new to this browser or newer than your copy. Untick any you do not want, then tap Restore. Restoring adds to what is already there and never overwrites a newer copy.
  4. To back up one session on its own, tap Back up on it under Previous sessions. The file is named after the session and the date, for example pennio-studio-market-chant-2026-11-15-1805.json. A full backup is named with the date, the time and how many sessions it holds.
  5. To find a backup file on your phone, open Files or Downloads and search pennio-studio. Your backup files, at the bottom of Sessions, lists every backup this browser has downloaded, with its file name, date and the sessions inside, so you know which one to pick.
  6. Give each session a type and a project in the Current session card in Sessions. Types are Song, Beat only, Jingle or ad, Chant or oríkì, Score for video and Practice. A project is any name you choose, such as an EP or a client, and every session with that name sits together. A new session starts in the same project and type as the one you were in.
  7. Under Previous sessions, tap a project to see only its sessions, pick a type to see only that type, or turn on Group by project. Each project heading shows its sessions and size, and Back up project saves just that project to one file, named after it.
  8. Under Previous sessions you can also search by name, project, key or BPM; sort by last worked, newest started, oldest, name, time spent or size; and filter to sessions with a recording, with checkpoints, or not backed up. Each session shows its size and when it was last backed up. Your sort and filters are remembered.

09Ojú Gbooro casting

Ojú Gbooro is a hair culture brand bringing the greeting to hairdressers back into everyday life, and bringing African hair back as culture. A ń wá onídìrí ìbílẹ̀ tó dáńgájíá: we are looking for skilled traditional hairdressers, and for models to wear their work.

The entry form is at pennio.agency/oju-gbooro, linked from the bottom of the studio. Pick Hairdresser, Model or Both, add how to reach you and a link to your photos, and send. Entries are open to people aged 18 or older.

10When something goes wrong

  • No sound. Turn the volume up, flip the iPhone silent switch off, and tap play again.
  • Record does nothing. Your browser has blocked the microphone. Allow it in the browser's site settings, or use your phone's recorder and Add recording.
  • "That file could not be read". Save the recording as .m4a, .mp3 or .wav and try again.
  • The drum reads the wrong tone. Record closer to the phone in a quieter room, calibrate in the Write tab, and correct syllables by tapping them.
  • My sessions are gone. You may be in a different browser, a private window, or your browser data was cleared. If cloud saving was on, sign in again from Sessions and it comes back. If not, restore your latest backup file. Without either, PENNIO has no copy to recover it from.
  • The sign-in email has not arrived. Check spam, wait a minute, and tap Send again. Sign-in emails are limited to a few per hour, so space out repeat requests.

Still stuck? Email contact@pennio.agency and say what you tapped and what happened.
