Before You Start

The product is rarely the problem.
What the customer sees is.

Most of the businesses I work with already sell something people want. What holds the price down is everything the customer meets before they buy: a logo that could belong to anyone, a page that reads like a template, a price with an apology attached. None of that is a product fault, and no amount of ad spend fixes it. Spending more only puts the same impression in front of more people.

This is the check I run on a business before I take the work on. Fifteen statements across three things: the look, the page, and the price. Tick a box only if it is true today, not if it is true once the thing you have been meaning to fix is fixed.

How to use this

Read each statement. Tick it only if it is true right now, with no qualification. Count your ticks at the end and read the last page.

The look

What a customer decides about your price before you have said a word.

01
Our logo would still make sense with the name taken off it, and it would not fit a competitor's business just as well.
02
We chose our colours and typefaces for a reason we can say out loud, not because they looked good on a moodboard.
03
Someone who saw our Instagram, then our website, then our packaging, would know it was the same business without being told.
04
A new designer could open our files today and know what is allowed and what is not, without asking us.
05
Nothing we currently use was made by copying a competitor's layout and putting our name on it.

The page

What happens in the first five seconds after someone actually arrives.

06
A stranger can tell what we sell and who it is for within five seconds on our homepage.
07
There is at least one thing on the site a customer can check: a named client, a finished job, a photograph of real work.
08
The page loads and works on a cheap phone on mobile data, which is how most of our customers will see it.
09
Nothing on the site is a placeholder, a dead link, or a "coming soon" that has been sitting there over a month.
10
There is one obvious next step, and it is the same one on every page.

Part 03

The price

Whether the business can hold its number when someone pushes back.

11
We can say our price out loud without adding a reason, an apology, or a discount in the same sentence.
12
We have raised our price in the last 18 months and kept the customers we wanted to keep.
13
We know who we are not for, and we turn those enquiries down instead of dropping the price to win them.
14
Our headline makes a claim someone could argue with, rather than resting on quality, excellence, or passion.
15
With our name removed from our last five posts, a regular customer could still tell it was us.
