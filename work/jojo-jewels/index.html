Jójò Jewels

Jewellery, retail · Identity, packaging and campaign

A fine jewellery house that needed to look owned rather than assembled, from the mark through to the box it ships in.

What was going wrong

The pieces were good. Everything around them had been picked up one at a time, from whoever was available when it was needed, so the tag, the pouch and the posts each looked like a different house.

For jewellery that matters more than in most categories. A customer who is about to spend real money reads the packaging as evidence of whether the thing inside is worth it.

The question that shaped everything

What has to be true of a mark that sits on a clasp a few millimetres wide and on a billboard in the same week?

Most jewellery marks answer one of those and fail the other. A drawing with fine detail dies at small sizes; a plain wordmark survives everywhere and says nothing.

The decision

I drew the mark as a ring, read as a monogram, so the product and the letterform are the same shape rather than two ideas sharing a page.

The detail went into the counters: the two O counters carry a four-point sparkle. That is small enough to emboss on a clasp and bold enough to hold at billboard scale, which is what let one mark cover the whole range instead of needing a simplified version for small sizes.

What I made

Positioning, the mark, the lockups in vertical, horizontal and icon form, a palette of forest, gold, sage and chalk, and the packaging and campaign work built on them.

It shipped as sixteen boards and a print document, so the next person to touch the brand has the reasoning, not only the files.
